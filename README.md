## What is USDT?

USDT, also known as Tether, is a stablecoin designed to track the value of the US dollar. It is commonly used as a trading pair, a way to move value between platforms, and a temporary holding asset inside a crypto portfolio.

USDT is not the same thing as money held in a bank account. Its price is intended to stay close to one US dollar, but the amount you receive, the exchange rate, fees, and withdrawal costs still depend on the platform, payment method, trading pair, and blockchain network.

There is also more than one version of USDT. It can exist on networks such as Ethereum, Tron, Solana, and other supported chains. The token name may be the same, but the network is not interchangeable in every situation.

That detail matters when sending USDT. If you withdraw USDT over one network and the receiving wallet expects another, the funds may not arrive correctly and recovery may not be possible.

## How to buy USDT with a spot order

A spot order is useful when you already have funds on OKX or want more control over the price.

There are two order types most beginners encounter.

### Market order

A market order attempts to fill immediately at the best available prices in the order book.

It is useful when:

- You care more about immediate execution than the exact price.
- The order is relatively small.
- The market has sufficient liquidity.
- You have reviewed the estimated amount and fee.

The final price can move slightly between order placement and execution. A large market order can also be filled across multiple prices.

### Limit order

A limit order lets you specify the maximum price you are willing to pay. The order may remain open until a seller accepts that price.

It is useful when:

- You want greater price control.
- You are not in a rush.
- You understand that the order may not fill.
- You are watching a liquid trading pair.

A limit order that enters the order book may be treated as a maker order. An order that executes immediately against existing orders is treated as a taker order. OKX explains that maker and taker fees are calculated differently, and the applicable rate depends on the account’s fee tier and trading pair.

For a simple first purchase, the buy interface may be easier than the advanced trading screen. For larger purchases or repeated transactions, the spot market gives you more information about price, liquidity, spread, and order execution.
